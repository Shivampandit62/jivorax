Useful information

Compound interest is the reason long-term investing works: each period's interest joins the principal, so the next period earns interest on a slightly bigger base. The effect is invisible at first and dramatic over decades.

The formula

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t) — P the principal, r the annual rate as a decimal, n compounding periods per year, t years. Compound interest is A − P. With ₹100,000 at 8% compounded monthly for 5 years, the result is about ₹148,985, of which ₹48,985 is interest.

Frequency matters less than you think

Going from yearly to monthly compounding at 8% adds roughly half a percentage point of effective yield. Going from monthly to daily adds far less still. The rate and the time in the market dwarf the frequency choice — but it's still worth matching your product's actual terms.

Effective yield and the Rule of 72

The effective annual yield folds intra-year compounding into one number so you can compare products directly. And for a quick doubling estimate, divide 72 by your annual rate: at 8%, money doubles in roughly 9 years.

Frequently asked questions

What is the compound interest formula?

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t), where P is the principal, r the annual rate as a decimal, n the number of compounding periods per year and t the time in years. Compound interest is A − P.

How does compounding frequency affect the result?

More frequent compounding earns interest on interest sooner, so the total rises slightly with frequency. Monthly beats quarterly, quarterly beats yearly — but the difference shrinks as the rate or term grows.

What is effective annual yield?

The actual yearly gain once compounding inside the year is counted: (1 + r ÷ n)^n − 1. At 12% compounded monthly it is about 12.68%, which is why the effective yield is higher than the advertised rate.

What is the Rule of 72?

A quick mental shortcut: divide 72 by the annual return to estimate how many years it takes to double. At 8% per year, 72 ÷ 8 = about 9 years. It's an approximation, not an exact figure.

Are my numbers stored?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser — your amounts are never sent to a server or saved by Jivora.
