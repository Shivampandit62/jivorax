How to use this converter

  1. Select your images. Drag several JPG, PNG or WebP files onto the dropzone at once (or click to browse). They are listed in the order they'll appear as pages.
  2. Pick a page size. Fit to A4 centres each image on a standard sheet — landscape or portrait to match the picture. Original image size uses the image's exact pixel dimensions with no scaling.
  3. Press Create PDF. The tool shows progress while it embeds each image, then reports the page count and final file size.
  4. Download. Save the PDF and open it in any viewer or send it to a printer. Need different settings? Change them and build again from the same selection.

Useful information

A PDF is the universal hand-in format: one file, same pages on every device, ready to print or upload to a portal. This tool builds one directly from your images — no office suite, no upload, no watermark.

How the pages are built

Each image becomes exactly one page, in selection order. JPGs are embedded byte for byte — the original compressed data is copied into the document, so there is no second round of quality loss. PNG and WebP files are rasterised on a canvas (at the resolution cap you choose) and compressed into the page; transparent pixels are filled with white so they print correctly.

A4 fit vs original size

Fit to A4 scales each image to sit centred on 595 × 842 points, flipping to landscape for wide pictures — print it and it lands where you expect. Original image size makes the page exactly as big as the pixels (72 pixels per point), which is right when another tool or archive needs the image untouched.

Keeping the file small

The resolution cap only affects PNG and WebP — JPGs keep their original bytes because re-encoding them would only add loss. If the PDF comes out heavy, lower the cap or compress the PNGs first with the Image Compressor.

What it doesn't do

There's no page numbering, text overlay, merging of existing PDFs or OCR — pages are images, exactly as you supplied them. The tool does exactly what it says and nothing more.

Privacy: reading, encoding and document assembly all happen in this tab. Your images never reach a server.

Frequently asked questions

Which images can I convert?

JPG, PNG and WebP files, up to 20 images at a time (20 MB each). Each image becomes one page of the PDF, in the order you selected them.

How are the pages sized?

Fit to A4 centres each image on a standard A4 page — landscape or portrait depending on the picture — ready to print. Original image size uses the image's own pixel dimensions as the page, with no scaling at all.

Are my images uploaded to make the PDF?

No. The document is assembled byte by byte inside this tab: your images are read, embedded and written into the PDF on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Why can the PDF be smaller than the images?

JPGs are embedded byte for byte — no re-encoding, so no quality loss. PNG and WebP images are drawn on a canvas at the chosen resolution and compressed into the PDF; transparent areas are filled with white. That is where the savings come from.

How big can the PDF get?

Photo-heavy documents grow quickly because PDF pages store pixels as images. Twenty large photos can produce a file of tens of megabytes — lower the resolution cap or split the document if you need it smaller.
